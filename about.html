About Us

A church's care for families, grown into a statewide foundation.

Onesimus works alongside people in the justice system, their families, and the professionals who support them — nurturing connection during incarceration and building a path back into community life.

Where we started

Onesimus had its genesis in the Christian Family Centre.

A local church adjacent to Risdon Prison, the Christian Family Centre supported children and families of offenders through its CFC Care Services long before Onesimus existed as a separate foundation. That relationship continues today — Onesimus and CFC remain close partners, and much of the Foundation's early family-support work, including the Regional Visit Centre network, grew directly out of church-based care.

What "Onesimus" means

Useful, or profitable.

A Greek name from the biblical book of Philemon: a man once written off as useless finds, through a chance meeting in prison, a way back to being valued by the people around him. It's the story this Foundation takes its name from.

Our vision

Creating healthy relationships. Transforming lives. Changing communities.

Our mission

We work with people with convictions, their families, and communities to create healthy family relationships during incarceration — so that on release, they are welcomed home as useful, profitable, and fulfilled members of society, with a reduced risk of reoffending.

A Fellowship that changed government policy

From a 2014 Churchill Fellowship to a 2016 state corrections strategy.

Norm Reed's study of 21 prisons overseas led directly to the "Breaking the Cycle" strategy and a new Family Worker role in Risdon Prison.
