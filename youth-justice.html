For professionals · Youth justice

Keeping children out of the justice system, and helping those already in it.

Onesimus works mainly with adults in the justice system and their families. Youth justice is part of the same picture. This section gathers practical, evidence-informed material on youth justice for practitioners, policy makers and anyone working with young people in Tasmania.

10

Ways to improve juvenile justice

Ten evidence-informed directions for keeping children out of the justice system, and helping those already in it.

"One of the biggest predictors of future imprisonment is age of first imprisonment. There are alternatives."
Rob White
Emeritus Distinguished Professor of Criminology, University of Tasmania
One-page handout (PDF)
  1. 01

    Justice reinvestment

    Invest in the community rather than in prisons.
  2. 02

    Restorative justice

    Put repairing harm at the centre of justice processes.
  3. 03

    Child- and youth-centred

    Put children and young people at the centre of the process.
  4. 04

    Therapeutic jurisprudence

    Address underlying problems and co-morbidities.
  5. 05

    Trauma-informed approaches

    Recognise and respond to grief and pain.
  6. 06

    Mentoring

    Link people to those they respect and will emulate.
  7. 07

    Raise the age of criminal responsibility

    Keep people out of criminal justice.
  8. 08

    Systems of accountability

    Rights protection.
  9. 09

    Victim engagement

    Mechanisms for active participation and meeting needs.
  10. 10

    Integrated offender management

    Provide extensive throughcare support.
Why Onesimus is involved

The adult system starts with the youth system.

Onesimus works where the justice system meets families and communities. Youth justice sits squarely in that space: the earlier a young person is drawn into custody, the harder the road back tends to be, for them and for the people around them.

We share this material to support informed discussion among practitioners. We are grateful to Professor Rob White for making his work available here.
