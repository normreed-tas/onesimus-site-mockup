What the numbers say

A prison sentence reaches into families. How far is barely counted.

Official figure
1 in 334%

people entering prison have children in the community who depend on them for their basic needs.

AIHW, The health of people in Australia's prisons 2025. Australia-wide.
Official figure
1 in 521%

people entering prison had a parent or carer in prison when they were a child.

AIHW, The health of people in Australia's prisons 2025. Australia-wide.
Research estimate
40,000+

children in Australia have a parent in prison.

Monash University study for SHINE for Kids, 2023. An estimate, not a count.

What isn't known

No one counts these children. Australian prisons do not routinely record whether the people they receive have children, so there is no official figure — nationally or for Tasmania. The national number above is an estimate, and no Tasmanian figure has been published.

"There's no official process to identify this group of children."Catherine Flynn, Monash University, Monash Lens, 2022

The AIHW figures come from a 2025 survey of people entering prison in every state and territory except Queensland. They are national, not Tasmanian. An earlier AIHW report (2022) found 40% had dependent children; we use the newer figure. For the official figures on prisons and youth justice in Tasmania, see Tasmanian justice figures.

Track record
What community has made possible

Onesimus has been building programs alongside families and prisons for over a decade. Some of this work continues; some belongs to a particular season — but all of it shows what's possible.

2012

Kids Days

A revived Father's Day programme, based on the UK's "Kids VIP" model, offering themed family visit days across Risdon's prisons — later run in partnership with Save the Children.

c. 2012

Hillside Haven

A house built on the church property to accommodate families travelling from regional Tasmania to visit a loved one in prison — inexpensive accommodation that let families double their number of visits without the round trip in a single day.

2013

Video Visits & Regional Visit Centres

A network of visit centres hosted in churches across Tasmania and interstate, connecting families who couldn't travel to the prison with an incarcerated loved one.

2016

Ron Barwick Playground

A $50,000 playground funded by the Tasmanian Community Fund, letting incarcerated fathers watch their children play during visits.

Ongoing

Onesimus Bear Program

Recording an offender's voice in the paw of a teddy bear — a small, tangible way for a child to stay connected to an incarcerated parent.

Ongoing

Second Bite & Loaves and Fishes

A prison garden where offenders grow produce to give back to the community — developed with TPS as a way to "pay back" through constructive work.

Ongoing

Church-facilitated care packages

Toiletry packs for women entering prison, pamper packs for women leaving, and clothing for men leaving — coordinated through a national network of churches.

Ongoing

Student placements & volunteer FEWs

Onesimus has long provided placements for university criminology and social work students, many of whom volunteer as Family Engagement Workers and go on to careers in the justice system. India O'Dowd volunteered as a near-full-time FEW in 2018 while completing her Honours in Criminology — she later joined Tasmania Prison Service as a Planning and Reintegration Officer, became a Correctional Officer, and is today an Acting Superintendent, leading the response to the "Constrained to Capable" report.

A track record with government

How one Fellowship became a Tasmanian government strategy

2014

Churchill Fellowship

Norm Reed visits 21 prisons across Singapore, France, Belgium, England, Wales and Scotland to study family-support practice.

2014–16

Hidden Sentence Training

Findings become a training program, engaging over 1,000 professionals in understanding families of offenders.

2016

Breaking the Cycle

Hidden Sentence is adopted as a key strategy in the Department of Justice's "Breaking the Cycle" corrections plan.

2016

Family Worker role

Tasmania Prison Service introduces a Family Worker role in Risdon Prison — a direct outcome of the Fellowship's findings.
