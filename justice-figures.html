Tasmanian justice figures

The official numbers, plainly.

Straight answers to questions about adult prisons, community corrections and policing in Tasmania. Pick a question and you get the figure, what it really counts, what to watch out for, and a link to the government report it came from.

Figures on young people are on the Youth Justice page. Where an adult figure has a youth equivalent, the answer links straight to it.

Why these sources give different numbers

Both sources are official, current and correct. They disagree because they count different things, and knowing which question you are asking decides which one to use.

The same question, asked two ways: how many people are in Tasmanian prisons?

784

ROGS: the average number held each day across the whole of 2024–25.

797

ABS: a headcount of adult prisoners held by corrective services on the single night of 30 June 2025.

Neither is wrong. An average across a year smooths out the peaks; a headcount catches one particular night, including whatever was unusual about it.

ROGS is for comparing — mostly. It provides a common framework for comparing justice systems across the states: what things cost, how full the prisons are, how many people come back. Published each January; its youth justice section is on the Youth Justice page. But the completeness and comparability of individual measures vary, so always check the qualifications on each answer.

ABS is for describing. Two publications sit here. One counts everyone in prison on a single night in June and says what they are held for. The other counts everyone police dealt with across the whole year, adults and children alike.

Small numbers jump about. Tasmania holds under 800 adults in prison. Thirty adults either way moves the imprisonment rate by about six and a half per 100,000. A change from one year to the next often means less than it looks.

Counting methods change. When a government improves how it counts something, the new number cannot be compared with the old one. Tasmania changed several counting methods in 2024–25. Where that matters, it is flagged on the answer.
