Practitioner's Guide

Supporting children and families affected by a family member's offending.

A practice guide for anyone who works with these children — in schools, health, child safety, community services, courts and corrections. Free, Tasmanian, and written for the worker in the room rather than the policy shelf.

Where it comes from

Built in Britain, rebuilt for Tasmania, and ours to keep current.

The guide began as a Barnardo's publication for the National Information Centre on Children of Offenders, written by Polly Wright and Lindsay Sutherland. Onesimus adapted it for Australian use with the Tasmanian CAPO working group, with funding through the Salvation Army's Communities for Children programme. It was printed and widely distributed, and became the text behind our Hidden Sentence training.

Onesimus holds written permission to reproduce and modify it. That matters: it means this guide can be kept current rather than left to age.

The practice core

Four chapters that have not dated

The practice in the 2018 edition holds up. The Tasmanian detail around it does not — phone numbers, departments, the shape of the prison's family work. We are rewriting the detail and keeping the practice.

International practice

Where Tasmanian practice differs from what works elsewhere, the rewritten guide says so. Our Churchill Fellowship took in twenty-one prisons across Singapore, France, Belgium, England, Wales and Scotland. Those notes sit alongside the Tasmanian text, sparingly, so a worker can see that the local arrangement is a choice rather than a law of nature.

Tools

The parts practitioners actually photocopy

These are the pages that came back from training rooms dog-eared. They work as well on a phone in a family's lounge room as they did on paper.

Parent / Carer Support Wheel

A structured way into what a carer needs, developed through CAPO. Badged to the Tasmanian project, not inherited.

Child / Young Person Support Wheel

The same instrument from the child's side — the one most used in Hidden Sentence training.

Dudley the Dog

A way for a younger child to say what they feel about a parent being away, without having to say it directly.

Supporting a child to visit a prison

A flowchart for the worker: what to settle before a visit, what to prepare the child for, what to do afterwards.

Children's and parents' FAQs

The questions that are actually asked — is it my fault, is he in a cage, what do I tell the school — with answers.

Stages of the criminal justice system

Arrest through to release as six stages, each with what a family needs at that point. Being redrawn for Tasmania.

Mockup note. On the published site each tool is its own page — usable on a phone, printable if a worker prefers paper, and linked from the chapter that explains it.

What we are working towards

Where this section is going

Onesimus is a custodian here rather than a principal provider. The aim is that this material is available to whoever needs it, accurate, and not dependent on any one organisation continuing to hold it.

Now

Confirm and consolidate

  • Current Tasmanian practice confirmed with Tasmania Prison Service
  • Source register completed across the CAPO archive
  • Resource appendices checked or rebuilt
Next

Publish the practice core

  • Chapters 2 to 5 rewritten in Tasmanian terms, with international notes
  • Each tool as its own page, usable on a phone
  • The 2018 edition kept alongside as the archived record
Then

Training that travels

  • Recorded sessions built on the guide
  • Material offered to TPS, Community Corrections and the community sector for their own staff
  • The generational thread connected to our youth justice work

Onesimus is not currently delivering direct services to children of prisoners. What we hold is the practice knowledge, the published guide, and the relationships to keep both current. If your organisation wants any of it, ask — that is what it is for.
